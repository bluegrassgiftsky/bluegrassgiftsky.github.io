I Spent Three Weeks Picking a Kitchen Sign and I Have No Regrets (Okay, Maybe One)

Notes from the workshop · Bluegrass Gifts

Let me paint you a picture. It is a Tuesday evening. I am standing in my kitchen in socks, holding a tape measure I don't know how to read correctly, staring at a blank wall above the coffee station like it personally insulted me. My husband has already gone to bed. The dog is giving me that look. And I have seventeen browser tabs open, all in service of one deeply important question: what should the sign say.

Now, some people would argue that a kitchen wall sign is not a decision that warrants seventeen browser tabs and three weeks of deliberation. Those people are wrong, and I suspect their homes are joyless. A kitchen sign is not decoration. It is a mission statement. It is the first thing you read when you shuffle in at six in the morning, half-asleep, reaching for the coffeepot like it's a life raft. That sign had better say something worth reading.

My first instinct was something inspirational. You know the type — a little cursive, a little 'gather here' energy, maybe a sunflower. I entertained this idea for about four days before I realized I am not an inspirational person before nine a.m. I am a sarcastic person who has probably already eaten something she shouldn't have. The decor needed to reflect that.

My sister suggested I go the sentimental route. Family recipes, coordinates of the house, our last name in big block letters. Sweet idea. Genuinely. But here's the thing about putting your last name on your kitchen wall in big block letters — it starts to feel a little like you're branding yourself, and I already have enough identity pressure from my LinkedIn profile, thank you.

So I landed, eventually, where I probably always was going to land: funny. Specifically, honest-funny. The kind of humor that isn't trying to be a joke so much as it is just... accurately describing the situation. And the situation in my kitchen is that snacking happens here, it happens often, and it happens with very little premeditation. When I found that carved snaccident sign, I genuinely laughed out loud at my phone, which startled the dog, which knocked over a glass of water. It was chaos. It was perfect. The sign was ordered within the hour.

Now here is the one regret I mentioned in the title. While I was browsing around, I also found this absolutely unhinged piece of carved wood wisdom — a sign that says hangovers last only a day, which is philosophically correct and also extremely funny — and I almost ordered it too. For the bar cart area. Which we have, in the corner of the living room, because we are adults who make our own decisions. I did not order it. I showed restraint. And I have thought about it at least twice a week since.

The kitchen sign arrived and it is, for the record, genuinely beautiful. I was a little worried going in because I'd ordered printed signs before that looked great on a screen and arrived looking like someone had sneezed a font onto a piece of particleboard. This one is actually carved — you can feel the letters with your finger, which I do more than I'd like to admit, just because it's satisfying. It hangs above the coffee station now, right where I can see it at six in the morning. It is accurate. It brings me joy.

The moral of the story, if there is one, is that you should decorate your home to reflect who you actually are rather than who you imagine a lifestyle magazine might want you to be. If you are a person who eats cheese over the sink at midnight, you deserve a kitchen that acknowledges and respects that. If you are a person who occasionally makes choices on a Saturday night that your Sunday self will find questionable, your bar cart wall has feelings too. Decorate accordingly. Three weeks is maybe a little long for a sign decision, but I stand by the outcome completely.
