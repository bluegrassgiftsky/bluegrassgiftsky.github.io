The Great Kitchen Sign Debate of a Tuesday Afternoon I Will Never Get Back

Notes from the workshop · Bluegrass Gifts

I have spent a genuinely embarrassing amount of time trying to pick out a sign for my kitchen. Not a car. Not a mattress. A sign. A flat piece of wood with words on it. And yet there I was, forty-five minutes deep into an internal monologue about font choices, wondering where exactly my life had taken this particular turn.

It started innocently enough. My kitchen wall has this awkward blank stretch above the window — too high for a shelf, too small for a clock, too weird for a painting. Every time I walk in to pour my morning coffee I glance up at it and think, something should go there. So one afternoon I decided to fix it, the way you fix things: by sitting down with a laptop and promptly doing nothing productive for nearly an hour.

The categories I cycled through were something to behold. First I went through the inspirational phase — you know the signs. Live. Laugh. Love. Gather. The ones that sound like they were written by a motivational poster that got lost on its way to a corporate breakroom. I do not need my kitchen to give me a pep talk at seven in the morning. I need it to hold coffee and stay out of my way.

Then I swung to the farmhouse-rustic direction. Lots of roosters. Lots of references to biscuits and gravy. I have never once made biscuits and gravy from scratch and I am not about to lie to my wall about it.

Then I found the funny ones, and something in my brain finally settled down. There is a whole corner of the hand-carved sign world that has apparently decided to just say the quiet part loud, and I respect that enormously. The one that stopped me cold was this carved sign for moms who are just trying not to raise assholes — and I am not exaggerating when I say I laughed out loud alone in my house. Because that IS the job. That is the whole job described in one sentence, no roosters required.

I did not grow up in a house where you put anything irreverent on the walls. My grandmother had a cross-stitch of Proverbs 31 in the kitchen and a ceramic chicken on the counter and that was the full range of acceptable kitchen decor. So there is a small, petty part of me that finds enormous satisfaction in the idea of hanging something honest and a little bit ornery right there above the coffee maker where she would absolutely have seen it. Love you, Mamaw. You raised an asshole. Just one, though. Could be worse.

What finally convinced me — beyond the humor — was that these are actually carved. CNC-carved solid hardwood, not vinyl stuck to a board, not a printed piece of paper laminated and called rustic. You can feel the letters. That matters to me more than I thought it would until I started paying attention to it, because most of what gets sold as a wood sign is essentially a sticker on a plank. This is the real thing, which means it will still look right in ten years and not be peeling at the corners by Christmas.

I also fell into a rabbit hole reading their Valentine's Day guide — the saved by the sign guide — which I found genuinely funny even though Valentine's Day is months away, because the bit about gas station roses is just true and someone had to say it.

Anyway. The sign is ordered. The wall will no longer be blank. My grandmother is probably shaking her head somewhere and my kids will grow up either deeply embarrassed by it or completely immune to embarrassment, and honestly either outcome is fine by me. That is parenting. You do your best and you hang an honest sign about it and you pour another cup of coffee and you carry on.
