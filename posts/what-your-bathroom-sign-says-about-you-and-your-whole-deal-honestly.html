What Your Bathroom Sign Says About You (And Your Whole Deal, Honestly)

Notes from the workshop · Bluegrass Gifts

There is a theory I have been developing for about three years now, mostly while waiting for people to finish in the bathroom at various family gatherings. The theory is this: you can learn more about a household from their bathroom sign than from an hour of actual conversation. Maybe more than from a full background check. The bathroom sign is unguarded. Nobody overthinks it. They just put up whatever made them laugh at the store, and in doing so, they reveal everything.

Let me walk you through the taxonomy. First, there are the households with no bathroom sign at all. Just a mirror, some towels, a candle that has never been lit. These people are either very chic or they are managing something. You cannot tell which until you open the medicine cabinet, and you should not open the medicine cabinet.

Then you have the WASH YOUR HANDS crowd. Block letters, usually painted wood or enamel, sometimes with a little hand illustration. These are good people. Responsible people. People who got really serious about something in 2020 and never fully unwound from it. They want you to know they take hygiene seriously, and honestly? Respect. No notes.

A step further along the spectrum you get the inspirational quote bathroom. Live Laugh Love territory, or a long passage about how the ocean is a metaphor for something. I have questions about these households too, but affectionate ones. Someone in that house believes in things, and they want you to believe in things while you are washing your hands, and that is a kind impulse even if the execution is a little much.

But my personal favorites — the households I feel the most kinship with — are the ones who put up something genuinely funny. Not trying-too-hard funny. Just quietly, confidently funny. A sign that makes you stop mid-handwash and actually read it twice. The bathroom humor sign is a power move because it is completely sincere. You had to choose it. You had to hang it. You are telling every single person who visits your home that this is the vibe, take it or leave it.

My aunt has a carved wood sign in her downstairs bathroom that just says FARMHOUSE in big rustic letters, and then underneath in much smaller letters, ish. It is the most accurate description of her decorating style I have ever seen — she has the shiplap, she has the galvanized buckets, she has one actual rooster that lives outside and has opinions. The sign owns it. That kind of self-awareness is rare and should be celebrated. I looked it up later and found out it was a carved wood sign, not a printed or vinyl situation, which somehow makes it even better. There is something about the commitment of carving a joke into hardwood that elevates it. Anyway, for the record, that particular farmhouse-ish sign comes from a little Kentucky workshop if you ever want one of your own.

The straight FARMHOUSE sign without the ish is its own statement, by the way. That one says: I know what I am going for and I am going for it, no apologies. I respect that too. There is a solid carved version of it — a rustic carved FARMHOUSE word sign — that has the same honest, no-nonsense energy as the people who hang it. Bold choice. Committed choice.

Here is the thing about bathroom signs specifically, as opposed to signs anywhere else in the house: the bathroom is where people are alone for thirty seconds in the middle of your party. They are not performing. They are just standing there, existing, and then they look up and read whatever you put on the wall. It is arguably the most captive audience in your entire home. You could put literally anything there and they would read it. The people who put something funny there understand this. They are playing a long game. They are making a tiny, private joke with every single guest, one at a time, without even being in the room. I find this deeply admirable. It is hospitality at its most efficient.

So next time you are at someone's house and you find yourself in their bathroom, look at the wall. Or the lack of wall decor. It will tell you something true.
