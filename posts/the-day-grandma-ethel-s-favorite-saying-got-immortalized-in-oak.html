The Day Grandma Ethel's Favorite Saying Got Immortalized in Oak

Notes from the workshop · Bluegrass Gifts

My grandmother Ethel had a saying for everything. Burned the biscuits? That's just extra character, honey. Forgot where she put her reading glasses? They'll turn up when they're ready. But her absolute go-to, the phrase she deployed approximately forty times a day from roughly 1987 until she passed, was this: But first, coffee. She said it before church. She said it before funerals. She said it before her own doctor's appointments, standing in the kitchen in her housecoat waiting for the percolator like it owed her money. The woman was consistent, I'll give her that.

When we cleaned out her house after she was gone, we found seventeen coffee mugs. Seventeen. Some were novelty gifts, some were plain white diner-style things with chips in the rim, and one had a rooster on it for reasons nobody could explain. But there wasn't a single thing in that kitchen that captured her the way her words did. No object said Ethel like that phrase did. And that bothered me in a quiet, background-noise kind of way for about two years.

Here's the thing about grief that nobody warns you about: it doesn't always hit you at the funeral. Sometimes it hits you at nine in the morning on a Tuesday when you're pouring your own coffee and you think, she would've said it right now. But first, coffee. And you almost laugh, and then you almost cry, and then you just stand there holding your mug feeling like a complete fool.

My cousin Darla was the one who finally did something about it. She found a little Kentucky workshop that hand-carves signs out of real hardwood -- not printed, not vinyl, actually carved into the wood -- and she ordered one that said exactly what Grandma always said. When she sent me a photo of the finished piece, I had to sit down. There it was, routed into solid wood in letters you could run your fingers over: But first, coffee. Darla hung her version in her kitchen above the coffee maker, which is honestly the most Ethel placement imaginable. If you've ever wanted something like that for your own kitchen, I'll just mention that the carved coffee sign she ordered is still up on the site.

The funny part -- and there is always a funny part in our family -- is that this whole project made us start cataloguing Grandma Ethel's other greatest hits. The woman had opinions. She had a phrase for the specific kind of tired you get from bad decisions, a phrase for the stubbornness it takes to keep going anyway, and several phrases that probably shouldn't be carved into anything and displayed in a home where children visit. We got into a whole group text debate about which ones were appropriate for public display and which ones needed to stay in the family archives.

Which is how I ended up discovering, in the middle of that very ridiculous group text, that there exists in this world a magic mug -- the heat-activated kind where a design appears when you pour something hot in -- that reads Fueled by Caffeine and Questionable Life Choices. I nearly sent it to my mother as a joke. I still might. That caffeine and questionable life choices mug would have made Grandma Ethel cackle until she cried, which is honestly the highest compliment I can give anything.

The sign Darla ordered is still up in her kitchen. Every time I visit, I see it first thing when I walk in, before I even say hello, and I feel something that isn't quite sadness and isn't quite happiness but sits right between them in a way that feels true. Grandma Ethel never owned anything particularly fancy. She didn't need to. She had a saying that she meant every single morning for decades, and now it's carved in wood in her granddaughter's kitchen, and it's going to outlast all of us. That's not a bad legacy for a woman in a housecoat waiting on a percolator. That's actually pretty perfect.
