The Great Anniversary Sign Debate of Our Living Room

Notes from the workshop · Bluegrass Gifts

My cousin Dale and his wife Patrice have been married for eleven years, which is impressive by any measure but doubly impressive when you understand that these two cannot agree on a single thing. Not where to eat, not what temperature the thermostat should be set to, and apparently — as I learned at their anniversary party last spring — not what kind of sign should hang above their home bar.

Now, I should back up and explain that Dale and Patrice have a bar cart situation in their den that has slowly evolved into what I can only describe as a full lifestyle. It started with a bottle of bourbon and a set of rocks glasses. It is now a dedicated corner of the room with a neon light, a mini fridge, and enough whiskey to host a small country music festival. The wall above it, though, had been stubbornly, conspicuously bare for two years. Patrice said it needed something warm and funny. Dale said it needed something with meaning. They had reached a stalemate so total that the wall might as well have had crime scene tape across it.

For their anniversary, they each — independently, without telling the other — decided to finally solve the wall problem. As a gift. To each other. You can see where this is going.

Patrice had ordered a carved wood sign with a whiskey joke on it. Something along the lines of I should probably stop drinking, which, if you've spent five minutes with Dale, is genuinely funnier than it sounds. She'd found it at this little Kentucky workshop that does hand-carved hardwood pieces — not the printed vinyl stuff you see everywhere, actual CNC-carved wood — and she was delighted with herself. The funny carved bar sign she'd picked out was sitting wrapped in the closet for a week.

Dale, meanwhile, had ordered a motivational quote plaque. Actions prove who someone is. Rustic, carved, the kind of thing that looks serious and intentional on a wall. He was equally delighted with himself. He thought it would balance out what he called the den's chaotic energy, which, Dale, buddy, you built the chaotic energy.

They exchanged gifts at the party in front of about fourteen witnesses, which was either the best or worst decision depending on how you feel about public disagreements as entertainment. Patrice unwrapped her plaque first and stared at it for a solid four seconds before saying, very carefully, that it was beautiful. Dale opened his and burst out laughing. Then they looked at each other. Then at the wall. Then back at each other.

The argument that followed was, I want to be clear, completely good-natured and absolutely hilarious to everyone watching. Patrice's position was that a home bar should have personality, warmth, something that makes people laugh when they walk in. Dale's position was that a wall is a commitment and you should put something on it that means something. Both of these are reasonable positions! That is what made it so funny! They were both right!

They ended up hanging both signs. The motivational one went in the hallway, where, Dale admits, it actually looks better. The whiskey one went above the bar, where Patrice was correct that it is the first thing people laugh at when they walk into the den. Eleven years of marriage, two signs, zero casualties.

I tell this story because it is the most relatable home decor disagreement I have ever witnessed in person, and also because I have thought about it every single time someone asks me what to get a couple as a gift. The answer, apparently, is to let them argue about it. Or just buy two things and let them sort it out themselves. Either way, you're providing an experience, and that counts for something.

If you're curious, both pieces came from Bluegrass Gifts, a small Kentucky shop that does solid hardwood carved signs — the actions prove who someone is plaque is still making Dale feel very wise every time he walks past it, which might be the real gift.
