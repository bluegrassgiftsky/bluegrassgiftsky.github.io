Does Your Kitchen Sign Sound Like a Person or a Pamphlet?

Notes from the workshop · Bluegrass Gifts

My grandmother had a needlepoint on her kitchen wall that said, in faded pink thread, 'This kitchen is seasoned with love and a little bit of hollering.' Nobody bought that for her. She made it herself, sometime in the seventies, probably while watching The Price Is Right. And honestly? That one crooked little square of fabric told you more about her than a whole scrapbook could.

I think about that a lot when I see kitchen decor these days. You walk into somebody's house and there's a sign above the stove that says 'EAT.' Just that. One word. In a font that looks like it was designed by a committee. And listen, I get it — minimalism is a whole thing, I'm not here to yuck anybody's yum — but 'EAT' doesn't tell me anything about the person who lives there. It tells me they found something on clearance at HomeGoods and it fit the color scheme.

There's a real difference between a sign that fills wall space and a sign that fills in a detail about who you actually are. The best kitchen signs have a voice. They've got a little attitude, maybe a little warning. They're the kind of thing a guest reads and laughs and goes, 'Oh yeah, that tracks for you.' That's the whole ballgame right there.

I was poking around online recently, deep in one of those rabbit holes where you start looking for a birthday gift and end up reading about the history of cast iron, and I stumbled onto a fun guide to joyful cooking and kitchen style over on the Bluegrass Gifts blog. It's got this wonderful section about how your kitchen's personality should come through in the details — the tools you keep out, the things you hang on the wall, the general vibe you're projecting to anyone who wanders in looking for a snack. Felt like it was written by someone who actually cooks, not someone who just photographs food for Instagram. Worth a read if you're in the mood.

The thing that got me thinking about all this in the first place was a carved wood sign I came across — a pantry open 24/7 sign in solid hardwood — and it just cracked me up. Not because it's trying to be a joke, exactly, but because it's true. Every household has one person (it's usually me, I'll be honest) who treats the kitchen like a 24-hour diner. No judgment, just snacks. A sign like that isn't decoration. It's a confession. It's autobiography. It's the kind of thing that makes your family laugh because they already know.

And the fact that it's actually carved into wood — not printed, not vinyl, not a sticker that's gonna peel off by February — matters more than it sounds. There's something about a carved sign that commits. It's not a Post-it note. It's not a dry-erase board. When something is cut into solid hardwood, it's saying, 'This is who we are. We're not reconsidering this.' I find that deeply comforting, honestly. In a world of infinite options and constant updates, a carved wood sign is a little flag planted in the ground.

So if you're thinking about kitchen decor and you want something that sounds like your family instead of a catalog — something with a little personality baked in, something that might make your mother-in-law snort when she reads it — maybe skip the 'EAT' sign this time. Look for the one that sounds like something somebody in your house would actually say. Those are the ones that end up staying on the wall for thirty years, getting a little dusty, becoming part of the place. Just like my grandmother's needlepoint. Which, for the record, I would absolutely steal if any of my cousins weren't watching.
