The Great Kitchen Sign Debate That Nearly Ended My Marriage (Not Really, But Almost)

Notes from the workshop · Bluegrass Gifts

I have spent more time than any grown adult should admit standing in the middle of my kitchen, hands on hips, staring at a blank wall above the stove and thinking: what does this space say about us as a family? The answer, for about three years running, was nothing. It said absolutely nothing. It said we bought this house, we cook food here occasionally, and we have strong opinions about cabinet hardware but zero follow-through.

It started innocently enough. My wife decided we needed a sign. Not a printed thing, not one of those vinyl-letter deals that peels up at the corners after one good steam from the pasta pot. A real sign. Wood, carved, the kind that looks like somebody's grandfather made it in a shop out back. Fine. I agreed. I thought this would take maybe forty-five minutes.

Reader, it took four months.

The first problem was that we disagreed on tone. She wanted something warm and sentimental. I wanted something funny. She kept gravitating toward phrases that made me feel like I was walking into a bed and breakfast. I kept suggesting things that made her look at me the way you look at a dog who has just eaten something he absolutely should not have eaten.

We briefly considered a quote about family and food and gathering, which is lovely in theory but which I pointed out exists in approximately eleven million kitchens across the country. We considered a rooster. We discussed, at length, whether a rooster was charming or whether a rooster was the kind of decision you regret by February. We could not reach a consensus on the rooster.

At some point during month two, I went down a rabbit hole looking at what other people put on their walls, which is how I ended up on the Bluegrass Gifts website at eleven-thirty on a Tuesday night. They do hand-carved hardwood signs, CNC-carved into solid wood, not printed, not decaled, actual depth you can run your finger across. I got distracted from my kitchen mission almost immediately because I found this carved flip flops sign that would be absolutely perfect for my sister's lake house and spent twenty minutes thinking about her birthday instead of solving my own problem.

Meanwhile, on a completely separate evening, my wife had apparently been doing her own browsing and came to me holding her phone to show me something she loved. It was a three-word love declaration in carved wood, the Truly Madly Deeply sign, which I will admit is genuinely beautiful and which I also knew I could not put in the kitchen without my buddies never letting me hear the end of it. She agreed it was maybe more of a bedroom situation. We had made progress. This was progress.

What finally broke the deadlock, if you can believe it, was my mother-in-law. She came to visit, walked into the kitchen, looked at the blank wall, and said, and I quote: y'all need to put something up there, it looks like a waiting room. And something about hearing it from a third party just cut right through all the deliberation. We picked something that same weekend. Warm but not precious. Funny but not embarrassing. It's up now. It looks great.

The lesson here, if there is one, is that decorating with another person is less about taste and more about negotiation, and negotiation takes time, and that is okay. Also maybe loop in your mother-in-law earlier. She will not be diplomatic about the blank wall but she will be correct, and sometimes correct is exactly what you need. The rooster, for the record, did not make the final cut. But I am not ruling it out for the back porch. A man has to have something left to fight for.
