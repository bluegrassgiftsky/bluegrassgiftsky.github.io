What Your Bathroom Sign Says About You (An Extremely Scientific Study)

Notes from the workshop · Bluegrass Gifts

I have a theory. It started, as most of my theories do, while I was standing in someone else's bathroom at a dinner party, pretending to wash my hands for an unreasonably long time just so I could read all the signs on the wall. And I realized: the stuff people hang in their bathrooms tells you more about them than a two-hour conversation ever could.

Think about it. The living room is curated. The kitchen is functional. But the bathroom? The bathroom is where people let their weird flag fly. Nobody puts something ironic in the bathroom because they're trying to impress you. They put it there because they thought it was funny at eleven o'clock on a Tuesday and they never looked back.

Category one: the Inspirational Household. You know these folks. Their bathroom has something carved or painted in a lovely serif font about gratitude, mornings, or the importance of being present. And here's the thing — I don't even mean that sarcastically. There's something genuinely disarming about walking into a stranger's powder room and being greeted by something like look for the good in all moments on a handsome piece of wood. It resets you. You came in there to escape the small talk for ninety seconds and instead you leave feeling faintly better about the world. These are the people who bring homemade food to every potluck. You want them in your life.

Category two: the Comedians. These are my people, personally. Their bathroom sign is either a pun so bad it physically hurts, something borderline inappropriate for a room that guests will definitely enter, or a very specific joke that only makes sense if you know the family's whole history. The sign is doing real narrative work. I once spent four full minutes in a stranger's half bath trying to figure out the backstory behind a single framed needlepoint. Reader, I never did.

Category three: the Accidentally Profound. This is my personal favorite category, because it reveals a particular kind of chaotic wisdom. These households have a sign that was purchased as a joke — something sassy, something with a little edge, like one of those carved kitchen warnings that basically dares you to leave a mess — and over time it has become, against all odds, the household's actual philosophy. The kids grew up under it. The family absorbed it. Guests quote it. What started as a gag became a value system. There is something deeply American about this.

Category four: the No Sign People. I'm not going to say anything bad about you. I'm just going to note that you exist, that your bathroom is very clean, and that I will always wonder what you're hiding.

Here's what I think is actually happening with all of this: the bathroom is the one room in your house where you make a design choice with zero expectation of credit. Nobody's going to say wow, great bathroom sign concept at your dinner party. You put it there for yourself. Or you put it there because it made someone in your household laugh on the day you bought it, and that's reason enough. That's honestly kind of beautiful.

The signs that stick around — the ones that get rehung in the next apartment, the ones that survive two moves and a renovation — those are the ones that said something true. Funny or tender or both at once. Wood holds up better than paper, and a good joke holds up better than most things. I think people instinctively know this, which is why hand-carved wooden signs have never really gone out of style no matter what decade you're decorating in. A little Kentucky workshop called Bluegrass Gifts has been making exactly that kind of thing — solid hardwood, CNC-carved, built to actually last — and when I stumbled across their funny carved kitchen sign a while back, I thought yes, that is the taxonomy in action right there. Category three, no question.

Anyway. Next time you're at a party, spend an extra thirty seconds in the bathroom. Not in a weird way. Just long enough to read the signs. You'll learn more than you bargained for.
